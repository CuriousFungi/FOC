================================================================================
WHAT THIS FIX DOES
================================================================================

Back-EMF (counter-voltage) increases with speed:
  V_bemf = K_e × speed × pole_pairs

At high speed, need more voltage to overcome back-EMF:
  V_total = I×R + V_bemf

If K_e (flux linkage) is underestimated:
  → Controller thinks back-EMF is lower than reality
  → Doesn't apply enough voltage
  → Current drops, torque drops, motor stops

By increasing flux linkage constant:
  → Controller estimates higher back-EMF
  → Applies more voltage to compensate
  → Motor maintains current and torque at high speed

================================================================================
PROPER MEASUREMENT (30 minutes - do this after quick fix works)
================================================================================

METHOD 1: Back-EMF Measurement
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

1. Disconnect motor from driver (no power)
2. Connect oscilloscope to phase A and phase B
3. Spin motor by hand at known speed (or use drill)
4. Measure peak-to-peak voltage on scope
5. Calculate:

   Speed (rad/s) = measured RPM × 2π / 60
   Electrical speed = mechanical speed × 50 pole_pairs
   K_e = V_peak-to-peak / (√2 × electrical_speed)

Example:
  Measured: 10V peak-to-peak at 200 RPM
  Speed = 200 × 2π / 60 = 20.94 rad/s mechanical
  Elec speed = 20.94 × 50 = 1047 electrical rad/s
  K_e = 10 / (1.414 × 1047) = 0.00675 Wb


METHOD 2: Coast-Down Test
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

1. Run motor at high speed in software
2. Set voltage to 0 (let motor coast down)
3. Monitor g_as5048_angle and phase voltages
4. Measure generated voltage vs speed
5. Plot V_bemf vs speed, calculate K_e from slope

================================================================================
TROUBLESHOOTING
================================================================================

PROBLEM: Motor still stops at same speed after increasing flux linkage
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

DIAGNOSIS: Voltage saturation
CHECK: g_desired_voltage_q_before_clamp vs g_m_voltage_q_in_controller
If desired > actual → hitting voltage limit

SOLUTIONS:
1. Increase voltage limit (if supply supports it)
2. Increase flux linkage even more
3. Reduce maximum speed target
4. Check power supply sag under load


PROBLEM: Motor becomes unstable/oscillates after increasing flux linkage
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

DIAGNOSIS: Overcompensation or high PI gains
CHECK: g_feedback_voltage_correction oscillating

SOLUTIONS:
1. Reduce flux linkage slightly (try 0.008 instead of 0.010)
2. Reduce PI gains (Kp = 0.2, Ki = 1.0)
3. Add low-pass filter on velocity error


PROBLEM: Motor won't start at all after increasing flux linkage
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

DIAGNOSIS: Flux linkage too high for low speeds
CHECK: Startup current and voltage

SOLUTIONS:
1. Reduce flux linkage (try 0.007)
2. Check startup boost voltage (MIN_VOLTAGE_THRESHOLD)
3. May need different value at low vs high speed

================================================================================
PHYSICS EXPLANATION
================================================================================

Why higher flux linkage helps:

Motor equation at high speed:
  V_applied = I×R + ω×(L×I + Ψ_magnet)
              ↑       ↑
           resistive  back-EMF
             drop

Where:
  Ψ_magnet = PERM_MAGNET_FLUX_LINKAGE
  ω = electrical speed (rad/s)

At 30 rad/s mechanical with 50 pole pairs:
  ω_electrical = 1500 rad/s

If Ψ = 0.005 Wb:
  V_bemf = 1500 × 0.005 = 7.5V

If Ψ = 0.010 Wb:
  V_bemf = 1500 × 0.010 = 15.0V

Controller uses Ψ to predict back-EMF and apply correct voltage.
Wrong Ψ → wrong voltage → motor stops!
